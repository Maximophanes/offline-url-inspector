The [1 October 2012 daily-CSV announcement](https://blog.majestic.com/development/majestic-million-csv-daily/) identifies the same download filename and Majestic 12 as the provider. Our approved acquisition records identify a download from the direct public HTTPS CSV URL, rather than a subscription/API route, of `majestic_million.csv` on 22 October 2026 UTC. Its 79,648,765 bytes and 1,000,000 rows hash to `a5bafba77ae3586dac6ad563a3f374c2f18baca9297b9c7e5137022f0356ea8a`. The contemporaneous landing-page hash is `47ed2572ca8d3582f0982d9a294681942b2f28d18a8f2e16ee1d27f6df434803`. Current page dates are not substituted for this identity.

The [25 December 2011 announcement](https://blog.majestic.com/case-studies/million/) instead describes a one-off snapshot generated on 22 December 2011, offered as Excel/TXT archive files under CC BY-SA 3.0. That asset is not our input. There is no evidence of copying from it or of an inherited ShareAlike condition in the later daily CSV. The later BY grant is relied upon for this separately identified distribution, not as a retroactive relicensing of the 2011 files.

The [CC BY 3.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode), particularly sections 3, 4 and 8, authorizes reproduction, transformation and redistribution subject to its conditions. The [official deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/) confirms commercial reuse and prohibits additional legal/technological restrictions. Selecting 10,000 ranks, excluding suffix-only names, normalizing/deduplicating to 9,570 registrable domains and generating indexes are treated conservatively as modifications, with attribution and change notices retained. No exemption based on a supposedly insubstantial percentage is required or claimed. The 883-record spelling tier does not create another license.

### Additional terms and database-rights caveat

The [general site terms](https://majestic.com/company/terms) contain broad website-material restrictions and separate subscription provisions. Our input was the expressly licensed public CSV rather than scraped report pages or subscription/API output. The specific data grant supplies reuse permission; the current general terms do not identify an exception withdrawing that CSV grant. This is a reasoned scope interpretation, not a court ruling. Access-frequency guidance is respected: there is no new CSV download here and no runtime polling. Website graphics, branding and unrelated commercial data are not licensed through this analysis.

The earlier Stage 7 report correctly noted that the [Creative Commons database FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#how-does-the-treatment-of-sui-generis-database-rights-vary-in-prior-versions-of-cc-licenses) distinguishes 3.0 Unported from 4.0: it does not expressly license sui generis database rights. That fact is retained. It does not by itself establish that this use is prohibited. Importantly, Majestic's [22 November 2016 daily-list announcement](https://blog.majestic.com/development/alexa-top-1-million-sites-retired-heres-majestic-million/) expressly invites mirroring the list for others to download or query with attribution. This is additional publisher evidence of intended public redistribution, not merely a license label.

Taken together, the identified public CSV, contemporaneous BY notice, adaptation grant and express mirroring invitation establish a documented compliance path for independent audit. We infer that ordinary attributed redistribution of this transformed public list falls within the publisher's communicated permission. We do **not** claim that CC BY 3.0 silently contains the 4.0 database-rights grant, that all potential rights were waived, or that a jurisdiction-specific implied-license question has been judicially resolved. That interpretive caveat remains visible to the auditor; no concrete conflicting dataset-specific reservation or acquisition restriction was found. It is not a basis to demand bespoke permission as an automatic pre-audit condition. Contrary authoritative evidence would reopen the issue.

## Public Suffix List

The [official distribution page](https://publicsuffix.org/list/) identifies the GitHub source. The pinned raw list itself carries the MPL 2.0 notice, and its complete source, upstream commit/blob/hash, ICANN/PRIVATE markers and unchanged notices are preserved. Both sections use the same list license. Rule content was normalized to A-label arrays and used in reference normalization; the arrays remain covered data.

[MPL 2.0](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/) sections 3.1–3.4 require appropriate source availability, license information and notice preservation when distributing covered source/executable forms, including a larger work. The [Mozilla FAQ Q8, Q9, Q16 and Q17](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/FAQ/) explains source access and client-side distribution and permits combining executable/minified covered material with other code without automatically relicensing independent files. Applying that treatment to the compiled tables embedded in HTML is an explicit interpretation. The MPL does not require converting every independent module to MPL.

Candidate compliance is conservative: both HTML files include the exact preferred raw PSL, compiled tables, transformation source, full MPL text and preserved notices in **Data sources & licenses → Popular-domain references & suffix rules → Public Suffix List attribution & source availability**. No internet access is needed to obtain that text. The companion `source-materials.zip` includes all exact build/data inputs, source manifests, generators and instructions, with deterministic archive entries. Do not distribute a modified table while omitting its corresponding source/notice. Source-form covered material remains under MPL 2.0, with recipients' rights preserved. No new warranty is offered. The pinned notice's earlier “proposal” statement is retained as history and followed by the candidate's source-availability statement.

Official PSL test fixtures retain their separate CC0/public-domain notice; they are source/test material, not additional runtime rules. **Final packaging and the compiled/larger-work interpretation remain an independent audit acceptance item.** If that review finds any unresolved redistribution obligation, release stays blocked.
